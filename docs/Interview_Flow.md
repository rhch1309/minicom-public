#### Minicom Session
Weʼll provide a skeleton project (built in the framework you chose).
This exercise is pretty open-ended; you get to choose what features to prioritise, as one of the purposes of this session is to see how you prioritise and scope doing things. This is a prototype implementation so feel free to take shortcuts and be scrappy.

For the Minicom exercise, the chosen framework is *Django (SQLite persistence)*

We use the very basics of the frameworks, no advanced features, but it still might be helpful to run through a quick tutorial beforehand to help set up for the Minicom loop, weʼd like to make sure you can get the app and its dependencies running on your machine.
To help set up for the Minicom loop, weʼd like to make sure you can get the app and its dependencies running on your machine. This will allow things to run more smoothly for you on the day. To do that just download this public Minicom repo and run through the installation instructions. Please follow the README instructions for your preferred framework and make sure you can run its servers.
The frontend component of the project relies on vanilla Javascript with some jQuery sprinkled in.
Please note that the purpose of the code in the public Minicom is just to verify that the relevant dependencies are installed. On the day of the interview, we will give you a zipped version which contains the prototype for Minicom on which you will be building further.

The premise is simple: build a smaller version of the Intercom Messenger, dubbed “Minicom,” in a set of amount of time. After you get the basic functionality working, you can choose which features to build out and what parts of the product you would optimize. At the end, you demo your product and explain the reasons behind your product and design decisions, and even explain what else you’d build if you had more time. It’s always interesting to see where people focus their time and whether they care about getting something into customers’ hands quickly. 

###### Using AI during the Interview 
At Intercom, AI coding assistants are standard tools for shipping faster. We want to see how you use AI to increase your velocity while maintaining quality and understanding. Use of AI tools is mandatory, however

1. You must share your whole screen
2. You must clearly explain the plan before prompting AI 
3. You must be able to show the interviewer that you understand the code that has been generated 
4. You must talk through the code that has been generated 
5. You must demonstrate that you’re able to use the tool to get to a better outcome faster - not a low quality solution faster 
6. You must clearly explain your understanding of problems before using AI to debug them 
7. You are expected to be able to debug any issues without the use of AI assistance.

#### Critical Thinking
The purpose of this interview is to understand how you approach complex and ambiguous situations. Youʼll be presented with realistic scenarios involving product decisions, experiments, and unexpected outcomes.

Weʼre particularly interested in how you:
● Reason through trade-offs and draw appropriate inferences from available data
● Identify potential risks, flaws, or alternative approaches
● Use available data to inform decisions while considering its limitations.

To note: This interview is not about having the “rightˮ answer but about showcasing your ability to think critically, communicate your reasoning clearly, and navigate uncertainty thoughtfully.

#### Technical Design
This is a role play-style session where you'll be translating a series of product requirements into a data model. No need to revise UML, just simple boxes and lines are fine as long as the relationships are clear!
We'll use HackerRank to share a diagram of a basic system with you and then hand over the virtual marker and ask you to diagram how you would extend the system to support a new product requirement weʼll describe. Weʼll continue this with a series of additional product requirements that we'll chat through. Youʼre welcome to add new models, attributes or relationships, modify existing ones, or remove ones from the diagram. Use whatever diagramming style you want; as long as you clearly explain the design. We want to see how you go about designing a system with a general set of requirements, as you would at the beginning of a new project.

What we will evaluate:
● Translating product requirements into a data modelling problem: How well you understand the product behaviour and evolve the data models to enable it.
● Independent data modelling: How well you can design a data model to support the product requirements while managing complexity in the system.
● Scope control: How well you stay focused on solving the problem at hand.
● Effective communication: How you describe your solution as it evolves.

#### Senior Engineer Scope 
*Just for reference to know how a senior engineer function (or expected to function) at Fin (previously Intercom)*

1. Ownership
Owns bigger, more strategic projects at the team level.
Projects are often vague in their definition of done and bigger in size.
Will lead team projects - either from the front as an expert executor, or as a force multiplier coaching less experienced engineers.

2. Autonomy
Coaching from EMs and more senior Engineers is less frequent.
Trusted with autonomy over the successful outcomes of team projects. Check in’s become less about checking work and more about reporting status.

3. Role In Practice
The Senior Engineer embodies and is a role model for our R&D principles and is sometimes the most senior IC on a team.
Each unit of work contributed by a Senior Engineer is likely high leverage - getting a lot done through people, code or some combination of the two.
At this level, Engineers typically have a shape, usually excelling at 2 or 3 of the above competencies, possibly to the detriment of others.

4. Knowledge & Context
Understands direction and roadmap for a team deeply, collaborating and contributing where necessary.
Deep understanding of systems in one team or area. Knows how these fit into the broader Engineering ecosystem.

5. Strategy & Planning
Contributes to technical strategy at the team or strategic area level.
Works on team level problems, owning them to completion.
Sets and elicits clear definitions of done for all projects or problems.

6. Execution
Effectively executes on any technical challenge, project or issue in the teams’ remit.
Owns issues backlog and deliberately manages key risks. Leverages teammates to effectively close out issues and risks.

7. Growth
Proactively invests in skills that help grow as a technical leader, force multiplier or expert executor.
Teaches, mentors and coaches other Engineers.

8. Communications, Partnership & Influence
Habitually disseminates their mental models to other engineers, especially those on their team.
Capable of bringing a team or a room to a shared understanding of a problem or solution.
